We are cell biologists, neurobiologists, and evolutionary developmental biologists.

We study the cross talk between the nervous and immune systems, using zebrafish and cavefish to ask how microglia and macrophages build and maintain the brain and body across evolution, development, and aging.

What we study.

Full research
Disease

Microglia as contributors to neurological disease

Using large-scale CRISPR screens to define new contributions of microglia to neurodevelopmental disorders, beyond their known roles in neurodegeneration.

Read more
Evolution

Evolution of microglia and macrophages

Comparing surface and cave populations of the Mexican tetra to uncover naturally evolved differences in microglia and other tissue-resident macrophages.

Read more
Cell biology

Cell biology of microglia and macrophages

How microglia and macrophages remodel their organelles, from lysosomes and peroxisomes to the ER, Golgi, and mitochondria, when they face stress.

Read more
Physiology & behavior

Impact of microglia on animal physiology and behavior

Asking which circuits microglia shape during early development, and how those early events have lasting consequences for animal physiology and behavior.
